What it does

It watches without you.
It builds with you.

1

It watches SAM.gov for you

Every federal opportunity is posted in one place, and no small firm has anyone whose job is to read it. This checks it nightly against your NAICS codes and your set-aside. Only what fits reaches your dashboard.

2

It ranks what it finds

Each opportunity comes back with a percentage. It reads the solicitation, weighs it against what your firm has actually done, and tells you whether this is a strong match or a passable one. You decide whether to chase it.

3

It builds the SF330 with you

You choose the projects for this solicitation and it suggests the ones that fit. You pick the personnel and the subconsultants. It works through the sections and combines them into one file at the end.

4

It checks you against the solicitation

Before you submit, it confirms you comply with every requirement the solicitation sets and flags the ones you have missed. Correct them yourself, or tell it to.

The nightly watch

Stop reading solicitations at midnight

SAM.gov carries everything the federal government intends to buy, and a small firm has nobody sitting there reading it. This reads it overnight against your codes and your set-aside, scores what fits against your own record, and leaves one page on your desk. You make the call from that page instead of a pile of PDFs.

Before you send

Nothing goes out with a hole in it

Packages get thrown out for an unsigned form or a missing attachment, not for a weak team. The last pass reads the solicitation back against what you built and flags every requirement you have not met. Correct it yourself, or tell it to. Anything it cannot source stays a visible blank until a person fills it in.

Read the guardrails
Tailored to your firm

It learns how your firm actually talks.

Off-the-shelf tools state facts about you. This one is taught to make your argument.

It argues your case

Bid work far from home and a generic tool will helpfully note the distance, as though writing your competitor's rebuttal. Explain once how you cover national work and it carries that argument into every package after.

Titles do not have to match

The solicitation wants a CAD Technician. Yours is titled CAD Manager and has run CAD for fifteen years. A literal matcher tells you that you have nobody. Here you overrule it, put in the person you meant, and it holds.

It shows you your own gaps

Open a résumé and it lists what your record is missing: a licence never filed, a certification nobody entered. Fix it once and every package built after that has it.

Questions

Straight answers

What does it cost?

Two parts: a one-time setup that scales with how much history has to be loaded, and a flat monthly that keeps it running and includes support hours. How pricing works lays out the shape. You get your actual number in writing after the call, before any commitment.

Where does my data live?

On your machine. Not in anyone's cloud. There's no shared database and nothing leaves your building.

Does the AI make things up?

It's built not to. Anything it can't source becomes a visible blank, and it blocks the package until a person fills it in. Facts your records own get written back over anything it generated.

How much of my time does setup take?

The reading and extracting is machine work. Your time goes into the questions only your firm can answer. That is real hours, but a few sessions rather than a months-long project.

Is this a fit for us?

Best fit: small engineering, architecture and construction management firms with a set-aside, already filing SF330s and wanting to file more of them without hiring for it. Probably not a fit if you bid commercial RFPs, or you don't have a project history yet.

Can I see the technical detail?

Yes. The details page covers every part of the system, including what it cannot do and what you would be buying into.

Tell me how your shop runs a pursuit.

That is the whole first step. There is no deck and no demo script, and you are not obligated to anything.
