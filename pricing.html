HOW PRICING WORKS

Two numbers, and one of them
depends on your archive.

There is no price list here, and it is not a negotiating tactic. Half the cost is determined by something neither of us can see until we look at it.

A one-time setup

This is the real work, and it is the half that varies. Your projects, your people, their licences and certifications, your past performance ratings and who did what on which job all have to come out of old proposals and into a database that is actually correct.

A firm with twenty projects and eight people is not the same job as one with two hundred projects and sixty. That is the whole reason this number cannot be printed on a page.

A flat monthly

The nightly watch on SAM.gov, the updates, and a set number of support hours every month. Same figure every month regardless of how many pursuits you run through it.

Support past the included hours is billed hourly at a rate agreed up front. Most months nobody gets near it.

Why the setup drives it

Everything downstream inherits the database.

The first installation taught this the expensive way. A project sheet came out with the wrong city on it. Not because the system invented one, but because the wrong one had been entered years earlier and nobody had ever had a reason to look. A résumé went out short a licence that the engineer had held the whole time.

Neither of those is a software problem, and no amount of AI fixes them. Every résumé, every project sheet, every matrix and every narrative paragraph is generated from that record. If the record is wrong, the package is wrong, confidently and in triplicate.

So the setup is not data entry. It is reading your archive, reconciling what disagrees with itself, and asking you about the parts only you can settle. Get it right once and it stays right. That is what you are paying for, and it is why a bigger archive costs more.

What to weigh it against

The comparison is not other software.

A marketing hire

The usual answer to not having enough proposal capacity is a salary, plus benefits, plus the months it takes them to learn how you work.

The pursuits you skip

Most small firms pass on work they could win because nobody had the days. That cost never shows up on a ledger.

The ones thrown out

A package rejected for a missing signature cost you the entire effort, and you do not find out until after the deadline.

Getting a number

You get it in writing, before you commit to anything.

1

We talk

How your shop runs a pursuit today, what you bid on, and what is in your archive. Thirty minutes, no deck.

2

A walk through a working install, on real solicitations, so you are not buying a description.

3

You get the number

Setup and monthly, in writing, scoped to what we actually found in your archive. Then you decide.
